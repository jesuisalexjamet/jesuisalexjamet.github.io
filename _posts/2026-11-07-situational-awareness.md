Between 5 September and 6 November 2026 I sent eleven letters to a small group of friends and family who had asked me to keep them posted on what I was doing with my portfolio. I did not set out to write a series. The first one was meant to be a single note explaining why I was changing my mind about a market I had been long for two and a half years. Then the trade moved, and people asked questions, and the note became a habit.

Read end to end, those eleven letters are not a track record. They are a method. And the method is the only part worth publishing, because the returns are the part that will not repeat.

> **Core thesis:** Situational awareness is not the ability to predict the next move. It is the discipline of writing down what you believe, why you believe it, what you are doing about it, and what would prove you wrong — _before_ the market tells you. The letters are the ledger. The returns are a by-product of keeping it honestly.

## What the letters are not

Three disclaimers, because the genre invites overclaiming.

They are **not a prediction engine.** I did not call the top. I called a divergence I could see from where I sit, and I positioned for it. Those are different activities, and confusing them is how people lose money with a correct thesis.

They are **not conviction.** Every letter contains a hedge, a risk budget, or an explicit statement of what would make me wrong. That is not weakness of belief; it is the only form of belief that survives contact with a market.

They are **not a track record.** Eleven letters over nine weeks is one regime, one portfolio, and a sample size that a statistician would refuse to look at. What follows is a description of a process, not evidence that the process works.

## The method, in four moves

### 1. Read the physical layer, not the narrative

The September letter did not start from a chart. It started from a physical constraint: the AI buildout is a construction project before it is a software business, and construction projects consume electricity, copper, [transformers](https://www.gigaenergy.com/blog/lead-time-delays-for-data-centers), and land on a schedule that financial models do not respect.

That framing produced the first position change. If compute demand keeps growing, the scarce input is not GPUs — it is power. So: overweight energy and power infrastructure, and trim the companies whose capital expenditure is outrunning their organic cash flow. The letter put numbers on it: AI-related bond issuance of roughly $270B in 2025, [projected by Morgan Stanley to approach $570B in 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/morgan-stanley-forecasts-ai-debt-135325015.html), against [hyperscaler capex that has stopped being fundable out of operating cash](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html).

The same physical reading produced the Apple position. Apple spends [a couple of percent of revenue on capex](https://www.a16z.news/p/charts-of-the-week-apple-skips-capex) while the hyperscalers spend several times that share, and it owns [an installed base of 2.5 billion active devices](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/01/apple-reports-first-quarter-results/). Whatever happens to the model layer, the rent collector on the distribution layer is capital-light. That is an architectural argument, not a momentum one.

And it produced the gold position, for a reason that has nothing to do with gold: long-dated bond yields kept rising _through_ Treasury intervention, which is what debt monetisation looks like before it is called that. [The 1940s precedent](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/wwii-to-the-treasury-fed-accord) is not a metaphor. It is the last time a sovereign with this debt profile chose inflation over default.

### 2. Separate the technology from the business model

This is the distinction I return to most often. Most people in my field collapse it in one of two ways: dismissing the technology because the equities are expensive, or assuming the technology's success implies the equities' success.

AI as a technology is real, and will change how the world operates the way the internet and optical fibre did. That does not imply that the companies deploying it are viable. The dot-com bubble did not disprove the internet. [The NASDAQ fell 78% from its March 2000 peak by October 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-com_bubble), giving up every gain of the bubble, and many of the companies that went public to sell it did not survive. [Amazon fell as much as 95% and did not regain its 1999 high until 2009](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/18/dotcom-bubble-amazon-stock-lost-more-than-90percent-long-term-investors-still-got-rich.html); [Google listed in 2004](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1288776/000119312504143377/d424b4.htm), after the wreckage had cleared. Both are now among the largest companies in the world. The survivors were real, and so was the destruction.

The November letter on [Anthropic's IPO](https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec) makes the same argument with a specific company. Good operational margin, a genuinely serious research organisation, and still: I will not buy at the IPO. Not because the company is bad, but because the listing mechanism rewards insiders exiting into retail excitement, and because most ETFs will buy it anyway. Missing the first week of a listing is not missing the company.

The corollary matters more than the call: _being right about the technology and wrong about the equity is the normal case, not the exception._

### 3. Size the position before you size the story

The 9 September letter is the one I would keep if I could keep only one, because it is the least interesting to read and the most useful to have written.

The thesis was that structural deficits and Treasury auction supply would keep pushing term premia higher, so: short US Treasuries. The implementation was long put warrants on the 30-year, sized at roughly **1.0% of net asset value**, with a stated scale-out at 30-year yields of 5.50%–5.75% or a [MOVE index](https://developer.ice.com/fixed-income-data-services/catalog/ice-data-indices-move-index) spike above 130.

That is the whole discipline in one paragraph. The story could be as large as I wanted; the position could not. A 1% NAV risk budget means the trade can be wrong and I am still here to be right about something else. It also means the trade can be _right_ and the outcome is still legible — nobody has to guess what I was thinking, because I wrote it down before the outcome existed.

The same letter ends with a line I stand by: _this is definitely on the wilder side — please don't copy._ A letter that only contains conviction is marketing. A letter that contains the size, the exit, and the warning is a record.

### 4. Pre-commit the exits

Every position in the series had its exit written before it had a profit.

The bond trade had a scale-out band. The semiconductor ETF had a stop-loss placed in anticipation of the US midterms, weeks before the midterms. The semiconductor re-entry had a take-profit at €105 per share, then a second at €100. When the November election produced a blue sweep and the AI complex sold off, there was no decision to make in the moment — the decision had already been made by someone calmer, which is to say by me in September.

This is the part that looks like discipline and is actually just bookkeeping. Pre-committing exits converts a series of emotional events into a series of administrative ones.

## The autumn, in sequence

The letters are worth reading in order because the sequence is the argument.

**5 September.** The thesis change: overweight power, trim hyperscalers, long Apple, overweight gold. The stated reason is a divergence between physical compute constraints and tech balance sheets.

**6 September.** The actions, itemised: liquidate half the semiconductor ETF, redeploy 75% into short-dated government and corporate paper ([ERNE](https://www.ishares.com/uk/individual/en/products/258114/ishares-euro-ultrashort-bond-ucits-etf), ~2.39%), 15% into gold miners as a leveraged proxy on gold, 10% held as dry powder. A note that I might short Meta or SoftBank over the [StarGate](https://openai.com/index/announcing-the-stargate-project/) financing structure.

**9 September.** The bond trade, executed and sized at 1% NAV, with the scale-out band and the MOVE threshold.

**12 September.** [US inflation prints at 3.4% year on year](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112026.htm). The 30-year moves from 5.25% to 5.35% — ten basis points — and the trade is up 21.23%.

**26 September.** The 30-year at 5.49%. The trade is up 46.03% since inception. Bond vigilantes are pricing further hikes. By the end of the month the long bond [touches its highest yield since 2002](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/treasury-yields-bonds.html).

**3 October.** The 30-year at [5.623%](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026). I begin exiting in three tranches; two are done, at 54% and 78%, averaging 62.7%. Proceeds go to ERNE to preserve dry powder.

**14 October.** The position is closed. Total return: 60.9%.

**17 October.** AI securities sell off. A take-profit is placed on the semiconductor ETF at €105, a 2.7% drawdown from spot. A note that I am preparing a gold entry for the moment US monetary policy admits [fiscal dominance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_dominance).

**25 October.** The €105 take-profit fills; half the remaining semiconductor holding is offloaded. Portfolio composition disclosed: 13.6% bonds, 9.3% crypto, the rest equities and equity ETFs.

**2 November.** Anthropic's S-1. I will not buy the IPO, and I am reducing semiconductor exposure and parking cash in cash-like assets.

**6 November.** The midterms. Democrats take [the Senate, the House](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/2026-midterm-election-results-live-updates.html), and the governorships of Texas, Ohio, and Iowa, among others — states that were the intended sites of large data centre buildouts, in [an election fought partly on electricity bills](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/democrats-midterm-election-2026-anti-data-center-governors-texas-georgia-ohio-ai-infrastructure-big-tech-spending-moratorium-regulations/). Neo-cloud companies and [the special purpose vehicles holding their GPU-backed debt](https://qz.com/gpu-collateralized-debt-ai-neocloud-coreweave-financing-risks-050526) reprice violently. My last semiconductor shares, held for two and a half years, sell on a stop-loss placed weeks earlier, at an average gain of roughly 125%. Proceeds rotate into short-dated European paper at about 3.0%.

Nine weeks, one thesis, four instruments, and a sequence in which every decision was written down before its consequence.

## What I got wrong

The letters are more useful for their errors than their results, so:

**The tone was too apocalyptic.** "I have been sounding like a doomsayer for the past few months" is my own line, and it is fair. A thesis delivered as prophecy is harder to falsify and easier to dismiss. The version I would write now is flatter and more conditional.

**Being early is indistinguishable from being wrong until it isn't.** The de-risking began in September. The violent repricing arrived in November. For two months the position looked like a costly hedge against a collapse that kept not happening. That period is the actual test of a process, not the week it pays off.

**The bond trade worked; the thesis is still unproven.** A 60.9% return on a 1% NAV position is a good outcome for a small risk budget. It is not evidence that fiscal dominance has arrived. It is evidence that the trade was sized so that being wrong would have been survivable and being right would have been noticeable.

**I have no idea whether the November move is the beginning or a wobble.** The letter says so explicitly: _I genuinely hope that I am wrong writing this._ That sentence is not modesty. It is the honest state of the evidence.

## Why write it down at all

Because the ledger is the only part of this that compounds.

A thesis held privately can be revised silently. A thesis sent to four people cannot. Writing the letters forced three things that no amount of reading would have: a stated size, a stated exit, and a stated falsifier. Each of those is a place where a bad idea gets caught before it becomes an expensive one.

There is a second-order benefit that I did not expect. The letters became a record of _how_ I think, not just what I concluded. When the November selloff arrived, I could go back to September and check whether the reasoning still held, rather than reconstructing it from memory in the middle of a drawdown. Memory is a terrible archivist and an excellent forger.

And a third, less comfortable one: writing to people who trust your judgement is a commitment device. It is much harder to talk yourself into a bad position when you know you will have to describe it in plain language on Saturday morning.

## The point

The number people will take from this is 60.9%. The number I would keep is 1.0% — the risk budget that made the trade survivable, and the reason the outcome is a data point rather than a story about a lucky bet.

Situational awareness is not a feeling about where the market is going. It is a ledger: what you believe, why, how much, and what would prove you wrong. Keep it honestly and it will not make you right. It will make you legible — to the people who trust you, and, more importantly, to yourself six weeks later when the market has changed and your memory has not.

The letters are that ledger. The returns are what happened while I was keeping it.
